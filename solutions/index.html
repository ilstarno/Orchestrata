Call-center routing

To design routing, account for queue priority, agent skills and languages, and who is available as calls arrive. The worked call-center example covers three simultaneous queues with qualified agents; it does not route individual calls or connect to a phone system.

What the examples cover

These fixtures illustrate assignment within fixed windows using eligibility already supplied in the inputs. They do not by themselves build full rosters, sequence job-shop operations, route fleets, generate entire timetables, or schedule a live computing cluster.

Compare the city workloads

Adapting the idea to rentals

Start with each booking’s pickup and return times, location, and eligible equipment. For bookings in one overlapping window, test whether each can take a distinct unit. A rental workflow also needs maintenance holds, turnaround time, inventory across locations, and availability across many booking windows. There is no checked-in rental fixture or booking system.
