Ces exemples testent les règles indiquées sur des données d’exemple. Ils ne constituent ni un avis juridique ni une certification de conformité. Avant tout déploiement, vérifiez les règles locales en vigueur et utilisez des règles internes approuvées.

Capacité et contraintes par secteur

Liste de vérification pour la modélisation. Elle ne décrit pas les fonctions prises en charge.

Arrimage des conteneurs et capacité des navires

Affecter les conteneurs selon leurs dimensions, leur poids vérifié et l’ordre des escales. Le nombre total d’équivalents vingt pieds ne prouve ni la compatibilité des emplacements ni la faisabilité du chargement.

Liste détaillée et sources officielles (en anglais)

Decisions

  • Compare loading sequences and compatible slots across vessels and ports before proposing a stowage plan.
  • Keep containers with missing or inconsistent verified mass out of the ready-to-load plan.
  • Estimate rehandles and equipment time when berth arrival or discharge order changes.

Capacity to measure

  • Count 20- and 40-foot slot positions separately and record paired-slot conflicts. Total TEU does not establish physical fit.
  • Track metric tonnes per stack, hatch or deck zone; use vessel-specific structural and stability limits, not aggregate deadweight alone.
  • Count compatible powered reefer positions and simultaneous crane moves per hour. Include outages and shore power limits.

Constraints

  • Where SOLAS verified gross mass requirements apply, use the signed verified mass in the stowage process; a booking weight is not a substitute. Verification is a condition of loading, not authority to load.
  • Use vessel-approved Cargo Securing Manual limits and applicable cargo separation rules. A general IMO reference does not supply clearance for a specific ship or commodity.
  • Keep port discharge order, permissible hatch passage, and terminal crane restrictions separate from fleet preferences such as minimizing rehandles.

What can go wrong

  • An open slot has insufficient stack mass margin or the wrong container length.
  • An available reefer box position has no usable power outlet.
  • A revised port sequence traps containers behind later-discharge cargo or invalidates planned weight distribution.

Evidence to collect

  • Ship-approved bay plan with slot compatibility and blocked positions, mass ledger with verified gross mass and timestamps, and equipment windows.
  • Vessel-specific loading and stability calculation signed off through the vessel's approved procedures; a planner replay alone is not proof.

Réapprovisionnement et stockage alimentaire

Réserver des emplacements selon les dimensions et la charge admissible des palettes. Tenir compte du froid, des dates limites et des allées libres; vérifier les blocages de préparation.

Liste détaillée et sources officielles (en anglais)

Decisions

  • Place each lot in a usable slot and allocate replenishment windows without blocking aisles or picking faces.
  • Reserve temperature-controlled space and handling equipment as stock and trucks arrive in waves.
  • Compare a promotion against a normal day using peak-hour labor and dock demand, not just total distance.

Capacity to measure

  • Pallet positions by dimensions and load rating in kg; cubic metres and case facings do not establish weight capacity.
  • Available powered lift trucks and trained operator-minutes; clearance in metres at the equipment's required turning envelope.
  • Dock door-minutes, concurrent staging pallet positions and chilled or frozen positions by approved temperature band in °C.

Constraints

  • Apply local workplace rules on cleared aisles and stable storage. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.176 is a US baseline where it applies, not a global aisle width or forklift load rating.
  • Keep site-required lot rotation, segregation, shelf rules, spill zones and promotional displays separate from regulation.
  • For US transport covered by FDA sanitary transportation requirements, match the food safety specification and agreed responsibilities; transport rules alone do not define warehouse shelf life.

What can go wrong

  • The bin is empty but the loaded pallet cannot be placed safely by available equipment.
  • Promotion volume fills staging before the dock window opens, blocking the receiving path.
  • A chilled lot physically fits in an ambient bay, but the approved handling conditions cannot be met.

Evidence to collect

  • Rack and equipment rating records, bay dimensions, shift-qualified operators and scanned lot and expiry dates.
  • Distinct capacity and queue measurements for receiving, staging, storage and replenishment, with agreed temperature handling evidence.

Tournées et recharge des flottes électriques

Planifier ensemble véhicules, tournées et recharge. Vérifier l’énergie utile, la puissance des bornes, leur disponibilité et la réserve au départ.

Liste détaillée et sources officielles (en anglais)

Decisions

  • Assign routes to vehicles, then schedule charge slots against return times, departure deadlines and minimum reserve.
  • Compare charge sequencing versus added connectors or a service upgrade before attributing shortages to vehicle count.
  • Replan when a charger faults, a route runs late or consumption deviates from the duty-cycle estimate.

Capacity to measure

  • Vehicle energy in usable kWh and percent state of charge; connector output in kW and station occupancy in minutes.
  • Shared electrical service limits in kW or kVA as specified by the site engineer and utility, with building load in the same time intervals.
  • Route payload in kg and m³, driver and vehicle minutes, and depot parking positions. Include energy for auxiliary refrigeration or climate load.

Constraints

  • Treat connector, vehicle acceptance limits, site electrical capacity and approved engineering settings as hard input boundaries; US DOE fleet guidance does not authorize electrical work.
  • Model employer reserve and priority policy separately from legal vehicle load, road access and driver rules. Confirm jurisdiction, journey and vehicle applicability before evaluating.
  • Do not assume that advertised charger power is sustained or that sessions can overlap on one connector; model derating and charge curves.

What can go wrong

  • Several vehicles start at once and exceed the site headroom even though each connector is within its rating.
  • Available energy covers mean route demand but misses reserve after cold weather or credible route delay.
  • The vehicle finishes charging, but the connector only appears compatible in the inventory and is not approved for that model.

Evidence to collect

  • Route and energy telemetry, asset-to-connector compatibility matrix, session event logs, and approved utility/site headroom by interval.
  • Replay a charger outage and late depot return without letting an undercharged vehicle appear dispatchable.

Portes d’embarquement et services au sol

Affecter les avions aux portes ou postes compatibles en tenant compte des temps d’occupation, des distances nécessaires et des équipes disponibles. Comparer les effets d’arrivées tardives.

Liste détaillée et sources officielles (en anglais)

Decisions

  • Assign contact gates and remote stands by aircraft and handling readiness, preserving space for late arrivals.
  • Compare a nominal gate assignment with the resulting baggage, passenger and ground-team workload.
  • Evaluate multi-stand closures or an arrival wave while showing which demand needs manual handling.

Capacity to measure

  • Gate or stand occupancy in aircraft-minutes, apron frontage and clearances in metres, and equipment compatibility by aircraft family.
  • Passenger-processing capacity per 15 minutes by staffed checkpoint, corridor, bus, and eligible arrivals stream; a gate count is not passenger capacity.
  • Qualified ground-team and equipment minutes in the same location and time window, including transfer and reset time.

Constraints

  • An empty stand still needs airport-approved aircraft and adjacent-stand compatibility plus applicable airport conditions. FAA terminal planning guidance supports design work; it is not airport certification or approval.
  • Use local airport and airline rules for international arrivals handling, baggage reclaim, stand closures and service equipment. Carry these as separately versioned site inputs.
  • Protect mandatory buffers and handling dependencies. Only then compare preferences such as contact-gate use, walk distances and towing effort.

What can go wrong

  • Large aircraft can fit each stand individually but cannot occupy neighboring stands at the same time.
  • Moving a flight to a remote stand frees a gate but saturates the coach queue or ground-team schedule.
  • Gate assignment is feasible while baggage or eligible passenger processing is over capacity during the same arrival burst.

Evidence to collect

  • Airport-approved stand and adjacency matrix, terminal and baggage processing rates, ground equipment roster and staff qualifications.
  • Stale closure and adjacency data must hold a recommendation; replay the peak arrival bank and verify no shared resource is double booked.

Flottes de location et capacité des ateliers

Affecter les véhicules selon leur catégorie, leur disponibilité réelle et leur retour prévu. Vérifier séparément les créneaux d’atelier, les pièces et les transferts.

Liste détaillée et sources officielles (en anglais)

Decisions

  • Match contract-compatible vehicles to pickup windows, including return uncertainty and one-way transfers.
  • Schedule inspection, cleaning, maintenance and recall holds before any next handover.
  • Rebalance approved substitutes between branches without treating unavailable workshop resources as capacity.

Capacity to measure

  • Available vehicle-hours by branch, class and equipment; distinguish physically present assets from release-ready assets.
  • Workshop bay-hours by compatible lift rating and technician-hours by required skill, plus waiting parts and wash or preparation capacity.
  • Transfer driver and vehicle minutes, destination parking slots and booking slack after expected return.

  • Separate operator roadworthiness obligations from local contract promises and upgrade/substitute rules. DVSA guidance cited here is for commercial goods or passenger operators, not a universal rental law.
  • Use the applicable inspection and recall records; a calendar flag alone is not a roadworthy-release decision.
  • Allow an out-of-service asset to be moved for permitted servicing only if specifically authorized. Do not treat that movement as rental availability.

What can go wrong

  • An asset is assigned as soon as it returns but needs inspection, repairs or a recall remedy.
  • A substitution satisfies inventory count but fails class, capacity or contract compatibility.
  • A workshop has free bays but lacks a qualified technician or a required part, stranding several bookings.

Evidence to collect

  • Asset state history, booking and substitution contract, parts plan, and technician/bay compatibility.
  • Signed first-use or equivalent evidence where it applies, recall and rectification trace, and a separate handover-release event.
