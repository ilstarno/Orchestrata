Before a lead becomes a contact, check the account

Use account context, contact ownership, and repeat submissions to prevent duplicate or misassigned CRM contacts.

A lead and a contact answer different questions. The lead records the request that came in. The contact identifies a person your team already knows or is about to add. Keeping the two separate lets a team follow up on a new request without assuming every form submission represents a new person.

Start with an exact comparison

Keep a stable lead ID, the company or account reference, and a work email. Trim surrounding spaces; define whether your system compares addresses without case differences. Avoid collapsing plus aliases or removing dots without a provider-specific rule you can justify. Keep the submitted and comparison values separate when you need to explain why two records matched.

Contact lookupWhat to do
No exact email matchLet a qualified lead move forward as a candidate for a new contact.
One exact match on the same accountLink the contact. Do not create a second copy.
More than one exact match, or a match on a different accountStop and ask a person to check the records before linking or creating anything.

A shared inbox and a person who changed employers can both produce confusing matches. Ask for more information instead of changing another account’s data or assuming the newest lead is correct.

Lead assignment is not contact ownership

An unavailable rep can keep ownership of an existing contact, while an available rep takes the incoming lead. Record those as separate fields. A qualification check should also stand on its own: if the need, relevant person, or next step is still unknown, keep the lead open for follow-up rather than creating a contact just to clear the queue.

A retry should not add a record

Keep the same lead ID when retrying the same request. In an implementation that writes to a CRM, decide how the server records completed conversions and enforces uniqueness before letting two simultaneous submissions create records. Test both a lost response followed by a retry and two requests that arrive at once. Both should return the same contact reference without duplicating the record or its conversion audit event. Best-effort lookup alone cannot make those tests pass reliably.
